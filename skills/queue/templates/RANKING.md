# Why the order is what it is — <Project>

Standing reasoning behind `QUEUE.md`. Read on a re-rank, not on every claim — which is the whole
reason it is a separate file. `QUEUE.md` is rewritten on every claim and every close, by every
window, so prose parked there is re-read on each of those edits while changing perhaps once a week.

Record the reasoning that a row cannot carry: why a tier was chosen, which tie-breaker separated
two items, and which orderings are dependency rather than importance. A rank you cannot justify in
one sentence means the item's problem statement is too vague to rank.

## The shape of this backlog

One paragraph: what dominates the order here. A pipeline whose steps depend on each other is
ranked mostly by *a prerequisite outranks its dependent*; a mixed backlog of bugs and features is
ranked mostly by tier. Say which, because it tells the next reader what kind of argument to make.

## Item by item

- **NNNN — <title>.** Why it sits where it does, in a sentence or two. Name the neighbours it
  beat and the rule that separated them. The valuable half is the *non-obvious* placement — an
  item ranked above something that looks more urgent, or below something that looks smaller.

## What would change the order

The conditions that would make this ranking wrong. An external date arriving, a dependency
landing, an assumption turning out false. Written now, while the reasoning is fresh, so a later
re-rank is a check against these rather than a fresh argument from nothing.
